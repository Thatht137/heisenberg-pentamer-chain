# Thermal Evidence Figure

This is a new research figure, not a replacement or alteration of the original L=40 spectra.

**Caption.** Thermal response near physically retuned interference zeros and its momentum-acceptance limitation. (a) The minimum leading positive-frequency weight coefficient `C_min`, with real exchange retuning optimized separately at each temperature, in the spin-3/2 effective periodic chains with four and six cells. The spin-1/2 first-order operator cancels identically; the zero line represents that analytic result rather than a clipped numerical signal. (b) Full microscopic two-cell (ten physical spins) spectra at `T/Jeff=1.6`, along the respective leading retuning trajectories. Weights are normalized to each branch's value at `g=0.005`; the fitted slopes are 2.02549 and 4.00189 for quartet and doublet branches. Normalization does not indicate equal absolute intensities. (c) Positive-frequency weights averaged over the uniform interval `[q0-delta q,q0+delta q]`, divided by the ideal central-momentum weight, at `g=0.005,T/Jeff=1.6`. The physical central momenta are `q0=pi` and `3pi/5` for the quartet and doublet. The parameters are held at the central-momentum retuning. These two-cell acceptance calculations are not an instrument-resolution forecast or a long-chain limit.

Energy and normalization conventions: `J1=1`, `Jeff=beta*c1(alpha_root)^2`, `g=beta/Delta_local`, and probe normalization `1/sqrt(5N)`. Thermal traces use all Sz sectors. Panel (a) removes complete equal-energy blocks. Panels (b,c) retain only transitions with both microscopic states below `E0+Delta_local/2`, but use the full microscopic partition function. Panel (c) averages transition probabilities before plotting; it does not smooth an image. Line segments are visual guides between calculated points. No Gaussian smoothing, reflection averaging, nonlinear intensity mapping, or statistical error bars are used.

Editable PDF/SVG and raster PNG/TIFF are exported from the same matplotlib figure. The displayed labels are restricted to axes, panel letters, and curve identification. Method parameters are recorded here rather than in a lower-left figure footer.

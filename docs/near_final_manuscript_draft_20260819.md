**From local pentamer multiplets to collective spin dynamics in an odd-unit-cell Heisenberg chain**

## One-sentence claim

At the tested positive-$\alpha$ points, the isolated five-spin cell retains an effective spin-$1/2$ doublet, so positive inter-cell exchange supports an antiferromagnetic effective-chain description over the tested regime; the finite-size observables are consistent with, but do not by themselves establish, a globally gapless $c=1$ phase. Local reflection parity then selects which multiplets are visible in the dynamical response, while negative intra-cell exchange produces a finite-size momentum reconstruction, with crossover retained only as a working interpretation and no jointly supported thermodynamic phase boundary.

## Abstract

Clusterized quantum spin chains provide a setting in which local multiplets and collective many-body excitations can be connected directly. We investigate a spin-$1/2$ Heisenberg chain with a five-site unit cell using exact diagonalization, low-energy projection, finite-size density-matrix renormalization group calculations, entanglement diagnostics, correction-vector spectra, and real-time matrix-product-state dynamics. In the positive-coupling region, the isolated pentamer retains an even-parity spin-$1/2$ ground-state doublet. Projecting the inter-cell exchange onto this doublet yields an antiferromagnetic effective Heisenberg chain, and both two-pentamer exact diagonalization and finite-chain calculations show that the leading correction to the projected energy is quadratic in the inter-cell coupling. Matched-size entanglement estimates, near-vanishing finite-size spin gaps, algebraic correlations, and a dominant $q=\pi$ response are consistent with a $c=1$ effective-chain description at the tested positive-coupling points; the odd-cell constraint does not by itself prove a globally gapless $c=1$ phase. Reflection parity makes the lowest excited doublet dark at $q=\pi$, whereas spin-$3/2$ multiplets carry 84.1%--97.6% of the isolated-pentamer inelastic weight. Production correction-vector and time-evolution calculations agree at a representative point, and six full-momentum time-evolution runs at three parameter points pass independent time-step and spectral-quality checks. In the negative-$\alpha$ region, 96 parameter–size records at 48 coupling points were evaluated (89 accepted by the Practical V2 gates, 7 bounded non-boundary records). A further 29 $L=60,80$ refinement cells passed the complete numerical validation chain. The large-size data show a finite-size reconstruction of the dominant momentum from approximately $0.60\pi$ to $\pi$, but none of eight candidate intervals satisfies the predeclared momentum and finite-size-flow criteria simultaneously. We therefore report a finite-size momentum reconstruction and retain crossover as a working interpretation rather than identify a separately named thermodynamic phase. These results support an odd-unit-cell route from local multiplets to an effective-chain description and delineate which conclusions can, and cannot, be inherited from the corresponding tetramer problem.

## 1. Introduction

Clusterized quantum magnets interpolate between local few-spin physics and extended many-body behavior. This makes them useful for tracking how isolated-cluster multiplets evolve into dispersive collective excitations, how symmetry filters the observable response, and how a change in the number of spins per unit cell alters the allowed low-energy phases. The recent spin-$1/2$ tetramer study provides a particularly useful reference because it organizes static order, entanglement structure, and dynamical spectra within one coupling diagram [@li2026tetramer_verified]. A five-site unit cell, however, is not a small geometric modification of a four-site cell: it contains an odd number of half-integer spins, so Lieb--Schultz--Mattis-type constraints provide a relevant logical boundary on unique symmetric gapped ground states [@lieb1961; @ogata2019]. This does not by itself determine the phase diagram or establish a $c=1$ phase.

Here we study a Heisenberg pentamer chain with two intra-cell exchanges, $J_1$ and $J_2$, and an inter-cell exchange $J_3$. Isolated-pentamer spectroscopy provides useful cluster-level context, but the all-spin-$1/2$ open linear geometry here is not assumed to be the same as earlier mixed-spin or differently connected pentamers [@haraldsen2011]. We use $J_1=1$ and define $\alpha=J_2/J_1$ and $\beta=J_3/J_1$. Our primary aim is not to transfer tetramer phase labels to the pentamer model, but to determine which microscopic mechanism replaces them and which dynamical signatures survive. Three questions structure the analysis. First, what is the low-energy degree of freedom of an isolated pentamer? Second, how does positive inter-cell coupling organize these degrees of freedom in the extended chain? Third, which local multiplets remain visible after the momentum and reflection-parity selection rules are imposed?

The results support a simple organizing picture in the positive-coupling region. An even-parity spin-$1/2$ doublet survives on every pentamer and projects onto an antiferromagnetic effective spin chain. Static finite-size observables and calibrated entanglement estimates are consistent with a gapless $c=1$ effective-chain description at the tested points, while the odd-cell constraint alone does not guarantee gaplessness or $c=1$ [@calabrese2004; @descloizeaux1962_verified]. Reflection parity suppresses the lowest excited doublet at $q=\pi$ and transfers most local inelastic weight to spin-$3/2$ channels. Independently validated correction-vector and real-time calculations then connect these local selection rules to the collective spectrum. The negative-$\alpha$ region is qualitatively different: accepted large-size calculations show a finite-size reconstruction of the dominant momentum, but the momentum and dominant-ratio diagnostics do not select the same narrow intervals. We therefore report a finite-size momentum reconstruction and retain crossover as a working interpretation while withholding a thermodynamic phase label.

## 2. Model and numerical strategy

The Hamiltonian is

$$
H=J_1\sum_n\left(\mathbf S_{n,2}\cdot\mathbf S_{n,3}+\mathbf S_{n,3}\cdot\mathbf S_{n,4}\right)
+J_2\sum_n\left(\mathbf S_{n,1}\cdot\mathbf S_{n,2}+\mathbf S_{n,4}\cdot\mathbf S_{n,5}\right)
+J_3\sum_n\mathbf S_{n,5}\cdot\mathbf S_{n+1,1}.
$$

The isolated pentamer has a 32-dimensional Hilbert space. We diagonalize it completely and resolve every degenerate energy subspace by total spin and reflection parity. Spectral weights are evaluated with projectors onto complete multiplets, rather than with individual eigenvectors whose orientation inside a degenerate subspace is arbitrary. The low-energy doublet defines an effective spin $\mathbf T_n$ through $P\mathbf S_{n,a}P=c_a\mathbf T_n$, giving

$$
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=NE_5+J_{\mathrm{eff}}\sum_n\mathbf T_n\cdot\mathbf T_{n+1}+O(\beta^2),
\qquad J_{\mathrm{eff}}=\beta c_1c_5.
$$

Finite chains use open boundaries and $L=5N$ physical spins. Ground states are obtained in fixed total-$S^z$ sectors using the sector-specific initialization protocol, following the DMRG/MPS framework [@white1992; @schollwoeck2011]. A state enters the accepted data set only after the declared variance, truncation, sector, and file-integrity gates pass. The positive-region static matrix contains 105 accepted records at $L=40,60,80$. In the negative-region calculation, two product-state initializations are used for $S^z=0$, while the $S^z=1$ sector is evaluated from the fixed `center` reference. Its Practical V2 protocol requires variance per site no larger than $5\times10^{-8}$, final truncation error no larger than $10^{-9}$, $|S^z-S^z_{\mathrm{target}}|\le10^{-8}$, and a spin gap no smaller than $-10^{-8}$. The new Practical V2 cells use a cap of at most 64 sweeps; this cap does not describe every historical data set. Every accepted record passes remote and local checksums, non-overwriting transfer, and an independent validator. The separate $L=80$ Krylov-$8$ control passed its release hash, fetched-file checksums, and the Practical V2 validator at $(\alpha,\beta)=(-0.10,0.25)$ and $(-0.175,0.25)$; its $k=3\to8$ comparison is explicitly `audit_only`. The $k=8$ gaps are $7.277203948\times10^{-5}$ and $7.277205361\times10^{-5}$ at $\alpha=-0.10$, and $1.587107409\times10^{-4}$ and $1.587107429\times10^{-4}$ at $\alpha=-0.175$, for the `center` and `ferro_j2` $S^z=0$ starts, respectively. Relative to the old $k=3$ gaps, $2(\mathrm{new}-\mathrm{old})/(|\mathrm{new}|+|\mathrm{old}|)$ gives $31.59\%/43.80\%$ and $2.06\%/4.57\%$; the largest paired entropy drifts are $41.03\%/53.16\%$ and $2.99\%/6.69\%$. Here $k$ is the Krylov dimension, distinct from $\chi$, so this control does not test bond-dimension convergence. The two $S^z=0$ starts are an initialization check, whereas $S^z=1$ remains fixed to `center`, not an independent statistical replicate. These controls do not establish convergence of all observables, a new phase, or a globally single phase. Because the old solver setting remains sensitive and the $L=40,60$ records use $k=3$ whereas $L=80$ uses $k=8$, they are not combined as a final scaling sequence. The first launcher's CRLF failure is retained only as historical provenance; current release and validation records are listed in `results/research_progress/project_closeout_status_20260919.json` and `results/autoresearch_v5_20260920/static_krylov_controls_r2_comparison_20260920/comparison.json`.

The dynamical structure factor is computed by two independent routes. Correction-vector DMRG provides the primary fixed-frequency spectrum at $q=\pi$ [@kuehner1999], while real-time evolution and Fourier transformation provide full-momentum spectra and an independent cross-check using the tensor-network time-evolution framework [@vidal2004; @fishman2022]. The accepted correction-vector/time-evolution comparison is bounded to the historical $q=\pi$, $\eta=0.1$ slice and its finite-OBC normalization and spatial-window approximation; the full-momentum time-evolution handoff is a separate campaign, not a claim that correction vector has been accepted at every momentum. The correction-vector implementation reproduces the complete $L=10$ spectrum with relative $L_2$ error $8.52\times10^{-12}$. The production full-momentum campaign uses $L=40$, $t_{\max}=30$, 121 momentum points, 301 frequency points, and paired time steps $\Delta t=0.025$ and $0.0125$. Raw negative spectral weight is retained in the plotted matrices and tested rather than clipped.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 An odd pentamer retains a low-energy spin doublet

For all 100 sampled points with $0<\alpha\le1$, the isolated pentamer ground state is an even-reflection-parity spin-$1/2$ doublet. The lowest excited doublet is odd under reflection, whereas the lowest spin-$3/2$ multiplet is even. The ground-state doublet remains an active low-energy degree of freedom and prevents the five-spin cell from behaving as a fully quenched integer-spin cluster.

At $\alpha=0.14$, 0.23, and 0.33, the projected coupling satisfies $J_{\mathrm{eff}}/\beta=0.4396$, 0.4318, and 0.4189, respectively. Hence positive $\beta$ produces an antiferromagnetic effective spin-$1/2$ chain. The residual between the projected and exact ground-state energies scales with powers 1.9689--1.9915 for two coupled pentamers and 1.9483--1.9495 for complete chains with $N=8,12,16$, confirming that the leading systematic correction begins at $O(\beta^2)$.

### 3.2 Positive couplings support a single tested critical regime

The strict positive-region mesh contains 105 accepted records. Its $L=40,60,80$ finite-size diagnostic does not produce a stable interior crossing: all accepted interior size differences retain the same sign under leave-one-out and weakest-point audits. This is a bounded negative result. It supports the statement that the present data contain no resolved internal boundary in $0\le\alpha,\beta\le1$, but it does not prove that every thermodynamic point belongs to one phase.

The low-energy observables provide an independent interpretation. At $\alpha=0.23$ and weak inter-cell coupling, matched-size entanglement estimators give $c=1.0240$--1.0331 relative to an open spin-$1/2$ Heisenberg-chain oracle. Finite-size spin gaps extrapolate close to zero, the correlation envelope has exponents 0.95--0.98, and the static structure factor is dominated by $q=\pi$. Together these observables are consistent with a gapless antiferromagnetic effective chain rather than a gapped tetramer-like cluster phase.

### 3.3 Reflection parity selects the visible local excitation

The lowest excited doublet is not the lowest visible $q=\pi$ excitation. Its spectral weight is below $4.6\times10^{-27}$ at the three representative positive-$\alpha$ points because the $q=\pi$ spin operator is even under reflection while the target doublet is odd. The lowest even-parity spin-$3/2$ multiplet carries a weight of approximately 0.325--0.333, and all spin-$3/2$ multiplets together account for 84.1%--97.6% of the isolated-pentamer inelastic response. These projector-resolved results establish a symmetry-based branch prior without assuming that an isolated multiplet remains a sharp quasiparticle at finite $\beta$.

### 3.4 Production dynamics connects the local rules to the chain spectrum

At $(\alpha,\beta,L,q)=(0.23,0.5,40,\pi)$, eleven accepted correction-vector frequencies cover $0\le\omega/J_1\le1.5$ and pass the peak-bracketing, spectral-weight, and residual gates. The resulting spectrum agrees with the independently converged time-evolution result within the declared peak and integrated-weight budgets.

Full-momentum time evolution was then completed at $(\alpha,\beta)=(1,1)$, $(1,0.1)$, and $(0.23,0.5)$. All six individual runs and all three $\Delta t$/$\Delta t/2$ pairs passed. The global and $q=\pi$ peak drifts are 0, 0, and $0.01J_1$ for the uniform, weak-coupling, and agreement points; the corresponding low-frequency integrated-weight changes are $4.32\times10^{-5}$, $2.74\times10^{-5}$, and $1.97\times10^{-5}$. These spectra are therefore production evidence, not pilot data. Their interpretation remains pointwise: the three panels demonstrate the evolution of spectral organization and time-step robustness, not a dense dynamical phase diagram.

The full-$q$ manifests use $\eta=0.1$ for the uniform point and $\eta=0.2$ for the weak-coupling and agreement points, with `space_window=18` in all three cases; cross-panel linewidths and weak peaks are therefore not compared quantitatively across the different broadenings.

### 3.5 Negative intra-cell exchange produces a finite-size momentum reconstruction without a confirmed phase boundary

The accepted negative-$\alpha$ data show a reproducible change of the dominant structure-factor momentum. Deep in the scanned region, $q_{\mathrm{peak}}\simeq0.60\pi$; closer to $\alpha=0$, the maximum locks to $q=\pi$. Stage B evaluated 96 parameter–size records at 48 coupling points (89 accepted by the Practical V2 gates, 7 bounded non-boundary records). The accepted-only analysis identified eight candidate intervals across seven $\beta$ slices.

Stage C evaluated these intervals with 29 additional $L=60,80$ cells at 64 sweeps. All 29 cells passed the remote parent and child checksums, non-overwriting transfer, local checksum, and independent validator. Seven of the eight targets retain either a momentum reconstruction or a sign change in $\Delta R=R_{80}-R_{60}$ that meets the declared diagnostic criterion. However, no adjacent interval carries both signatures, and only the $\beta=0.05$ momentum interval reaches the target width of 0.025. The $\beta=0.10$ slice provides no supported internal interval at the final size pair.

The Practical V2 stopping line allowed the planned large-size data set to be evaluated without changing the truncation, sector, gap, or provenance requirements. This resolves the earlier batch-acceptance bottleneck, but it does not establish convergence of every observable. The two finite-size diagnostics do not identify a common narrow boundary. We therefore report a finite-size momentum reconstruction and retain crossover as a working interpretation, rather than claim an additional thermodynamic phase or a continuous two-dimensional phase boundary.

### 3.6 Saved-state diagnostics retain a localized entanglement anomaly

Six additional points were evaluated with saved ground-state wavefunctions at $L=80$, using two initial states and 64 sweeps per initial state. The points were $\alpha=-0.25,-0.175,-0.10$ at $\beta=0.25$ and $\alpha=-0.625,-0.50,-0.425$ at $\beta=0.75$. All six parent runs passed the Practical V2 validation. The largest energy variance per site was $1.2875\times10^{-9}$, below the frozen $5\times10^{-8}$ threshold. Numerical acceptance did not, however, determine the thermodynamic phase.

The earlier six-point $L=80$ audit used six parameter points and two initial states, hence 12 entropy profiles. Across four central-chain windows and both initial states, a fit with ten independent intercepts, resolving bond position and unit-cell parity, gave an effective entropy coefficient $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.467$--0.605 at $(\alpha,\beta)=(-0.10,0.25)$. At the same $\beta$, the corresponding ranges were 0.886--0.971 at $\alpha=-0.175$ and 0.981--1.049 at $\alpha=-0.25$. The three $\beta=0.75$ points gave ranges within 0.905--0.976. These ranges describe sensitivity to the fitting window and initial state, not statistical confidence intervals or significance estimates.

The reduced coefficient at $(-0.10,0.25)$ persisted in this alternative analysis. A subsequent current comparison combined ten new validated calculations with two reused $L=80$ endpoints, covering $\alpha=-0.175,-0.150,-0.125,-0.100$ at $\beta=0.25$ and $L=40,60,80$. All twelve physical points passed the Practical V2 validator and supplied 24 initial-state profiles. The current analysis retained 192 shared-slope fits, whereas the earlier six-point audit retained 336 fits under its broader shared-slope and separate-class design. These lengths correspond to only $N=L/5=8,12,16$ pentamers, respectively.

At $\alpha=-0.10$, the ten-intercept entropy coefficients span 0.970--1.340, 0.625--0.959, and 0.467--0.605 at increasing length. With the fitting window fixed to $f=0.20$, the dual-start mean decreases from 0.988 to 0.695 and 0.533. This strong size dependence cannot be identified with a thermodynamic central charge. At $L=80$, the two initial states give sector gaps of $4.6625\times10^{-5}J_1$ and $5.2920\times10^{-5}J_1$; their full difference is 12.65% of their mean despite both states passing numerical acceptance. This is an initial-state spread, not a statistical confidence interval or significance estimate. Observable-level convergence therefore remains a separate requirement.

We also compare the root-mean-square difference between same-class bonds in adjacent cells, restricted to pairs lying in the central half of the chain, and the connected intercell-bond correlation at separation $r/N=1/4$. Both diagnostics decrease over the available lengths at all four parameter points. These finite-size trends do not establish a nonzero long-range order parameter, but neither exclude one. The sector gap of an open chain is not assumed to be a bulk excitation gap. No additional phase or critical exponent is assigned from this twelve-point comparison.

### Reproducibility note: entropy sensitivity analysis

For each cut $b$, the recorded entropy was fitted as $S(b)=a_g+(c_{\mathrm{eff}}/6)x_b$, where $x_b=\log[(2L/\pi)\sin(\pi b/L)]$. The group index was either $g=b\bmod5$ or $g=b\bmod10$, with a shared slope and independent group intercepts. Unweighted least-squares fits used $fL\leq b\leq(1-f)L$ for $f=0.10,0.15,0.20,0.25$. The earlier six-point audit also retained separate fits for each of the five bond classes, whereas the current twelve-point comparison retained only the shared-slope period-5 and period-10 models. No fits were discarded using an $R^2$ threshold in either audit. The shared-slope models are diagnostic approximations, not established descriptions of every point.

The earlier six-point $L=80$ audit checked 12 source entropy profiles against their SHA256 manifests and produced 336 fits: six points times two initial states times four windows times two shared-slope models plus five separate-class fits. The present twelve-point comparison checks 24 profiles and produces 192 shared-slope fits. A synthetic profile with a known slope and group offsets tested coefficient recovery, and a rank-deficient design tested rejection of an unidentifiable fit. These tests check the fitting implementation, not the physical validity of the entropy model. This was a post hoc sensitivity analysis motivated by the initial anomaly; it is not an independent preregistered confirmation.

## 4. Discussion

The main contrast with the tetramer model follows from unit-cell parity. Four spin-$1/2$ degrees of freedom can form an integer-spin or singlet cluster sector, whereas five leave a half-integer degree of freedom unless additional symmetry breaking or degeneracy intervenes. More precisely, the odd-cell constraint can exclude a unique symmetry-preserving gapped ground state under the relevant translation and spin-rotation assumptions, but it does not by itself guarantee a globally gapless spectrum or $c=1$. In the tested positive-$\alpha$ pentamer points, the surviving doublet turns the inter-cell exchange into an antiferromagnetic interaction between effective spins. The tested finite-size observables are therefore consistent with a gapless $c=1$ effective-chain description, while the tetramer phase names and local triplon/quarton/quinton assignments cannot be transferred by geometric analogy alone.

Near $\alpha=0$ and fixed $\beta>0$, a separate controlled-limit analysis considers a trimer--dimer decomposition valid for $|\alpha|\ll\min(1,\beta)$. The trimer projection has a close effective-spin-$1/2$ analogue in the verified trimer-chain literature, but the five-site coefficients and dimer elimination below are this project's derivation [@cheng2022trimer]. Projecting the trimer doublets and eliminating the inter-cell dimer triplet gives the candidate bulk exchange $J_{\mathrm{eff}}=2\alpha^2/(9\beta)>0$. The 8-spin trimer--dimer--trimer oracle, evaluated at 47 points with 11 checks, supports this local exchange scaling and its small-$|\alpha|$ domain. It is not a calculation on the complete pentamer chain: defining $\epsilon=(\mathrm{gap}/\alpha^2-2/(9\beta))/(2/(9\beta))$, the finite-$\alpha$ controls at $(-0.10,0.25)$ and $(-0.175,0.25)$ give $\epsilon=-36.1351\%$ and $-53.8550\%$, respectively. These are signed deviations from the leading local $J_{\mathrm{eff}}$ term, not L80 numerical errors; the open-boundary sector gap need not equal a bulk gap. This mechanism therefore remains a bounded local interpretation, not evidence for a global $c=1$ phase or a second thermodynamic phase.

The spectral calculation adds information that a static phase diagram cannot provide. Reflection parity removes the lowest doublet from the local $q=\pi$ response, while the higher spin-$3/2$ sector remains bright. The production full-momentum spectra show that this local hierarchy coexists with a dispersive low-energy continuum and with substantial unit-cell folding or high-energy structure away from the uniform limit. Because the real-time window produces visible negative ringing, all spectral-quality checks were applied to the raw matrices. This makes the convergence claim auditable but also prevents overinterpretation of weak high-energy features.

The negative-$\alpha$ data show why enlarging the plotted domain does not automatically imply more phases. The accepted large-size data show a repeatable finite-size momentum reconstruction, but its apparent location drifts between $L=60$ and 80 for several slices, while the dominant-ratio sign changes often occur in neighboring rather than identical intervals. This mismatch is consistent with a broad finite-size crossover or a slowly converging incommensurate regime. It is not sufficient evidence for a Lifshitz boundary, and additional points of the same type would not resolve the missing agreement between observables.

## 5. Conclusion

At the tested positive-$\alpha$ points, the isolated pentamer retains an even-parity spin-$1/2$ doublet, positive inter-cell coupling generates an antiferromagnetic effective chain, and the leading projection error is quadratic in the inter-cell exchange. Calibrated entanglement, gap, correlation, and static-structure-factor data are consistent with a gapless $c=1$ effective-chain description at those tested points, but neither odd-cell parity nor these finite-size observables establishes a globally gapless $c=1$ phase. Reflection parity makes the lowest excited doublet dark at $q=\pi$ and transfers most isolated-pentamer inelastic weight to spin-$3/2$ multiplets. Correction-vector and real-time calculations agree at a representative point, and three full-momentum time-step pairs provide production-level dynamical spectra.

On the negative-$\alpha$ side, the completed accepted-only campaign reveals a reconstruction from $q_{\mathrm{peak}}\simeq0.60\pi$ to $q=\pi$. All 29 final large-size refinement cells pass the numerical and provenance gates, but none of the eight candidate intervals satisfies the momentum and dominant-ratio boundary criteria simultaneously. We therefore report a finite-size momentum reconstruction and retain crossover as a working interpretation, while deliberately withholding a new phase name. This separation between established mechanism, production dynamics, and bounded phase structure is the central evidential boundary of the present work.
